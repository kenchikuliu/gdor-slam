\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Protocols and Metrics}

We evaluate GDOR-SLAM on dynamic RGB-D sequences from TUM RGB-D~\cite{sturm2012benchmark} and Bonn RGB-D Dynamic~\cite{palazzolo2019bonn}.
Four evidence blocks are kept separate.

\textbf{DYN-15} is the primary same-source tracking protocol: Semantic, GDOR, and Strict are run with three seeds on TUM walking\_xyz and all six Bonn dynamic sequences, for 63 completed runs.
\textbf{DYN-18} is a separately sealed validation using the unchanged configurations on the four TUM sequences named in the DyPho-SLAM tracking table: walking\_xyz, walking\_halfsphere, walking\_static, and sitting\_halfsphere.
It contains 36 planned cells after one exact Semantic sitting seed-0 repair; the initial failure and repair are retained in the audit.
\textbf{DYN-17} evaluates the map-matched no-track-reuse control on three sequences and three seeds, for 9 completed runs.
\textbf{DYN-16} is a seed-0 common-view mapping diagnostic on TUM walking\_xyz and Bonn crowd, with 20 shared non-keyframe views per sequence.

All tracking runs process the full association file and retain invalid intervals in the all-input-frame trajectory.
We report SE(3)-aligned ATE RMSE, one-frame translational RPE RMSE, failure rate, and end-to-end wall time.
The mapping diagnostic evaluates PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on a fixed static-region mask shared by all compared configurations.

The benchmark harness stores the source snapshot, executable and library hashes, detector-engine hash, mask-configuration hash, association and ground-truth hashes, sequence, seed, command line, and output identity.
The archived campaigns contain per-frame tracking state, recovery, insertion-gate, trajectory, runtime, and manifest records.
The public release additionally contains the explicit overlap audit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:leak-audit}; the archived DYN-15--18 summaries predate that field and therefore do not provide an empirical zero-overlap count.
The DYN-18 protocol was sealed before evaluation; the one failed Semantic cell was repeated once without code or configuration changes in a fresh output root.
The failure and repaired result both remain registered.

Table~\ref{tab:main-tracking} shows two complementary results.
In DYN-15, GDOR reduces the All7 mean ATE from 11.331 cm to 3.798 cm, shifts the sequence-median ATE from 4.278 cm to 3.261 cm, and wins every declared sequence mean relative to Semantic (7/0/0 wins/ties/losses).
The largest rescue is Bonn crowd2, where the mean falls from 52.184 cm to 3.226 cm.
Across Bonn6, GDOR reduces the mean ATE by 68.2\%.
Because the mean is dominated by crowd2, Table~\ref{tab:main-tracking} reports the failure case and median next to the mean rather than using the average alone.

DYN-18 tests the frozen method on a separate TUM4 campaign.
GDOR improves all four sequence means over the same-source Semantic parent and wins 9 of 12 paired sequence/seed cells (9/0/3 wins/ties/losses).
The median paired improvement is 0.209 cm; the corresponding two-sided exact sign test is not significant ($p=0.146$).
The improvement is small on walking\_static and walking\_halfsphere, but large on sitting\_halfsphere, where Semantic suffers repeated tracking failures.
Sitting\_halfsphere contributes a 25.020 cm mean paired improvement and three wins, whereas the other nine cells yield six wins, a 0.102 cm mean improvement, and a 0.188 cm median improvement.
We therefore interpret DYN-18 as conditional rescue of a failure regime plus near-identity behavior elsewhere, not as a uniformly large effect.
Strict is slightly lower than GDOR on three DYN-18 rows, but it is not promoted to the main method because the broader DYN-15 protocol shows substantially worse mean ATE and higher runtime.

Table~\ref{tab:matched-control} addresses a central alternative explanation.
Matched applies the raw semantic-plus-flow support to both tracking and the persistent mapping weight, while GDOR keeps that mapping weight fixed and recovers only the qualified tracking support.
Across the three tested sequences, the matched control obtains 7.816 cm mean ATE, compared with 4.053 cm for GDOR.
The median sequence improvement is 0.864 cm, GDOR wins all three sequence means (3/0/0 wins/ties/losses), and the failure-case contrast is Bonn crowd2 at 13.597 cm for Matched versus 3.226 cm for GDOR.
Thus, a fixed raw-mask tracking route with the same persistent mapping weight does not explain the reported GDOR result on this subset.
The control does not separate temporal recovery from adaptive replenishment, but it isolates their combined tracking-side effect from a persistent mapping-weight change.

GDOR lowers the All7 failure rate but increases wall time from 32.46 s/run to 49.84 s/run.
The overhead comes from residual-flow estimation, temporal-depth maintenance, and feature bookkeeping.
Strict is slower and less stable in the broader protocol, which motivates the Guarded configuration as the accuracy--cost compromise.

Map support is not a quality score.
It is the mean fraction of processed-frame pixels satisfying $W_t^{\mathrm{map}}\ge0.5$.
Semantic supplies no static weight, so 100\% denotes the mapper's default unit weight rather than the static fraction of its tracking mask.
For GDOR and Strict, the value records the conservative persistent mapping boundary; recovered tracking observations do not alter that weight.
In the same DYN-15 audit, GDOR rejects 11.25\% of considered ORB insertions and 11.94\% of RGB-D Gaussian densification candidates at the static-mask boundary.
These rejection counters show that the mapping gates are active, but they are not substitutes for the overlap counter in Eq.~\eqref{eq:leak-audit}, which is unavailable in the archived summaries.

The DYN-16 common-view audit is a limited preservation diagnostic that checks whether this tracking intervention damages the declared static-region reconstruction views.
GDOR improves the two-sequence online mean from 19.068 to 20.154 dB PSNR, from 0.690 to 0.740 SSIM, and from 0.354 to 0.287 LPIPS.
Strict is marginally higher in PSNR.
Because this audit uses one seed, two scenes, fixed static masks, and no independent ghost/completeness truth, it supports only a limited preservation diagnostic, not zero ghost, zero contamination, or a general mapping-superiority claim.

\subsection{External Positioning and Limitations}

Published DGS-SLAM, DG-SLAM, and DyPho-SLAM values are retained as related-work context, not mixed with the local tables as protocol-matched reruns.
The DyPho-SLAM paper reports 1.6, 2.6, 0.6, and 1.6 cm on the four corresponding TUM sequences.
GDOR is not below those external values in DYN-18.
Differences in implementation, masks, preprocessing, hardware, and evaluation prevent a valid direct ranking.
An official protocol-matched DyPho-SLAM rerun is therefore required before claiming external superiority.

The second limitation is mapping breadth.
The current common-view evaluation is stricter than method-specific render selection because views and masks are shared, but it is single-seed and covers two scenes.
A multi-seed common-manifest mapping study is required for a quantitative mapping-superiority claim.
Finally, the experiments establish the combined GDOR mechanism and a matched persistent-weight/no-track-reuse control; they do not claim that any one internal cue independently causes the entire gain.
