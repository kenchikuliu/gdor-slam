\section{Conclusion}

GDOR-SLAM implements an auditable two-route contract for dynamic-region evidence in an RGB-D/ORB Gaussian SLAM pipeline.
Temporal depth, residual flow, and tracking-risk evidence qualify a conservative subset of excluded observations for transient localization, while persistent ORB and Gaussian mapping remain governed by the conservative map mask.
This separation allows tracking utility without automatic map-writing authority.

The completed DYN-19 package extends evaluation to 90 main runs, 120 ordered-ablation runs, and 72 multi-seed mapping cells.
Its results show conditional rescue of severe tracking failures rather than uniform improvement, and the non-monotonic ordered contrasts do not identify independent component effects.
The 30/30 Full certificates and the failing Full-NoM counterfactual support the implemented route invariant, while the mapping outputs remain diagnostic without independent ghost/completeness truth.
The earlier single-seed DYN-16 audit is retained only as a limited preservation diagnostic.

The current evidence supports the specific guarded recovery and routing mechanism; it does not claim conceptual priority for tracking--mapping separation, independent component causality, or universal dominance.
It does not establish superiority over protocol-matched external implementations or measured zero ghost contamination.
Eligible external reruns and independent revealed-background truth are the remaining requirements for broader claims.
