\begin{abstract}
Dynamic masks protect a persistent Gaussian map, but hard exclusion can remove background and camera-consistent observations that remain useful for localization.
This paper studies the resulting task mismatch: an observation that is useful for transient camera tracking is not necessarily eligible to modify a static world model.
We present \textbf{GDOR-SLAM}, which separately routes qualified dynamic-region evidence to current-frame tracking and conservative evidence to persistent ORB and Gaussian mapping.
The completed DYN-19 evaluation contains 210 tracking runs over ten TUM/Bonn sequences and 72 common-view mapping cells over four scenes and three seeds.
The Full route lowers the ten-sequence mean ATE from 10.119 to 4.930 cm relative to Semantic exclusion, with 19/30 paired-cell and 7/10 sequence-mean wins, while reducing failure from 1.497\% to 0.147\%.
Every Full cell contains observed recovered support and passes an exact zero-overlap route certificate; a mapping-disabled counterfactual produces the expected overlap in all 30 cells.
Full also improves online static-region PSNR from 18.866 to 19.763 dB and GT-aligned static PSNR from 12.082 to 13.491 dB in the common-view diagnostic.
An ordered mechanism matrix identifies the simpler temporal-plus-mapping route at 3.859 cm mean ATE, but this consumed-data result is treated only as a candidate for a separately registered held-out test.
The evidence supports the implemented privilege separation and aggregate benefit with sequence-dependent failures; factorial causality, universal superiority, official DyPho-SLAM superiority, and independent ghost ground truth are not claimed.
\end{abstract}
